What it is

The Minnesota Rent Assurance & Deposit Protection Act is a working draft, written in public, that would create a state-chartered insurance-style pool for residential rental risk. It is drafted in four titles so each part can be read, priced, and advanced on its own strength — one act, one subject ("residential rental risk").

  • Title I — Deposit protection. Every security deposit moves to bonded custodial escrow for the tenancy; independent adjudication replaces the landlord's itemized return. Modeled on the UK's custodial tenancy deposit schemes (operating since 2007). Amends Minn. Stat. § 504B.178.
  • Title II — Rent assurance. A guarantee fund pays covered rent to the landlord during verified tenant hardship (job loss, medical crisis — defined trigger list), with no subrogation against the tenant. Recommended path: a pilot on publicly-subsidized housing first; pilot data becomes the fiscal note for statewide rollout.
  • Title III — Renter's belongings coverage. Contents coverage (fire/theft/water) as a third line on the same pool. Deferred pending actuarial — included so the structure is visible.
  • Title IV — Governance & review. Governing board with tenant and consumer seats; sunset with a mandatory year-3 report; public claims registry modeled on FCRA accuracy rules.

The funding braid — three sources

LegMechanismModeled share
Deposit float Yield above the statutory 1% on escrowed deposits (~3% net) ≈ $3.25 / unit / mo
MN Renter's Credit (M1PR) Monthly advance payment to the pool, reconciled on the renter's tax return (federal APTC / Form 8962 mechanism); enrollment rides the CRP e-file data rail; over-cap renters pay the share directly, disclosed ≈ $6 / unit / mo
Landlord matched share Community-rated base + bounded experience surcharge; clean-book years earn a no-claim dividend from surplus (mutual model) ≈ $6 / unit / mo

Split ratio is an open drafting question pending actuarial work. Base case ≈ $15.33/unit/mo at $1,300 rent ≈ 1.18% of rent.

Locked design calls

  1. No subrogation — a paid claim ends the matter; nothing is recovered from the tenant. This is the clause that separates insurance from the deposit-replacement products tenants are being hurt by today.
  2. Mandatory participation — funded by the three-source braid above.
  3. Independent claims adjudication + a tenant counter-claim lane — neither the landlord nor the carrier decides claims.
  4. Tenant/consumer seats on the governing board (anti-capture).
  5. Manufactured-claim penalties — surcharge plus referral, symmetric to penalties for knowingly-false fraud referrals against tenants.
  6. Public claims registry — aggregate per-owner data, FCRA-style accuracy and dispute rules.
  7. Sunset + mandatory year-3 report to the housing committees — a checkable promise.
  8. Renter's belongings coverage as a third leg — shape deferred pending actuarial.

Questions staff usually ask

Who administers it? Open drafting question. The current proposal: the fund at Minnesota Housing, adjudication at an independent body, licensed-insurer administration contracted out — awaiting confirmation.

Interaction with FHPAP emergency assistance? Open — the draft flags avoiding duplication.

Is the math real? Modeled and published, with the stress cases shown — but the premium math is explicitly pending actuarial review. The draft does not claim a number it can't back.

Single subject? Drafted as one act on one subject (residential rental risk); the credit-advance mechanics are written as machinery inside the act, not as separate tax policy.

What does year one look like? The recommended path is a pilot on publicly-subsidized properties without further appropriation; pilot data constitutes the fiscal note for statewide rollout.

Status, stated plainly

A working draft, written in public since October 2026. Not filed, no sponsor yet, and it may never become law. Every edit and every decision behind it is on the public record — see the version history. Questions from staff are answered in the open on the board.
